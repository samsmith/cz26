Help & FAQ
What is this app for?
This app lets you encrypt your thoughts, and then store them, such that only someone who knows three very difficult things can get in. The match has to be exact — a single wrong character, or a location link that reads even slightly differently from the one you used, and the decryption doesn't work.
How do the three setup answers work, and why those three?
All three are folded together into one key, and every piece has to be exactly right or none of it fits. But strength isn't the reason there are three of them — almost all of that comes from your passphrase. They're three because three unverifiable answers give you room to be plausibly wrong under pressure. See What if someone makes me open it? below.

What should I do if someone asks me what this app is?
It's for protecting your secrets, in a way that the universe will end before they can guess the keys. And if you don't want to share with someone, just give a different safe place, or a line from a poem instead of your safe phrase, and there is no way for anyone to tell.
What if someone makes me open it?
This is what the three questions are actually for. They aren't there to make the key harder to guess — a long passphrase does that on its own, and does it far better. They're there so that I can't get in is a believable thing for you to say.

Nobody can check your answers. There's no record anywhere of which day you feel safest, or which place, so there's nothing to catch you out against. And you don't have to invent a story and hold it under pressure: give the right place on a slightly wrong day and the app just doesn't open, exactly as it would if you'd honestly misremembered. You only have to be a little bit wrong.
How far does that actually protect me?
Worth knowing the edges before you lean on it.

A wrong answer gives you a failure, not a decoy — there's nothing in here that opens up to show someone a harmless version. So this protects what you wrote; it doesn't get you out of the room, and someone who doesn't believe you can keep asking.

It also doesn't hide the app. What's private is what you wrote, not the fact that you wrote something.

And the person most likely to pressure you is often the person most likely to know your safe day and your safe place. If that's your situation, pick answers they wouldn't land on, even if they feel less true to you. The questions are prompts, not a test — nothing checks whether your answer is sincere.
