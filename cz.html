Help & FAQ
What is this app for?
You write something down, and this app locks it up. Only someone who knows your three answers can open it again.

The answers have to match exactly. One wrong letter, or a map link that isn't quite the same as the one you used, and it stays shut.
How do the three setup answers work, and why those three?
All three are folded together into one key, and every piece has to be exactly right or none of it fits. But strength isn't the reason there are three of them — almost all of that comes from your passphrase. They're three because three unverifiable answers give you room to be plausibly wrong under pressure. See What if someone makes me open it? below.
What's the small pattern next to Help?
It comes from your answers, and nobody else's answers make yours. It won't change on its own.

You don't have to memorise it. You'll know your own well enough to notice when something isn't it, and noticing is all it's for. See What if the pattern next to Help looks different? below.
Can I forget or change my secrets later, or delete them now?

Yes. This forgets the key held on this phone and takes you back to the three questions, so you can set new answers or simply leave it unset.

It doesn't reach anything you've already copied out — that stays wherever you put it. But treat anything you encrypted before as closed for good: a fresh setup builds a fresh key, and the old one isn't kept anywhere.

Where does my message go after I press Done? is anything saved on this phone?
You can copy the encrypted text to the clipboard, and then paste it somewhere you can get it in future. that could be a random tumblr you've created, it could be a google doc that you have access to, you could email it to yourself. We won't do anything with it for you
What should I do if someone asks me what this app is?
Tell them it keeps your private things private. Nobody can guess the answers — there isn't enough time left in the universe to try them all.

And if you'd rather not let someone in, give a different safe place, or a different phrase. The app simply won't open, and nobody can tell whether you got it wrong on purpose.
What if someone makes me open it?
This is what the three questions are actually for (there's also a fourth hidden question which only matters when you come to decrypt). They aren't there to make the key harder to guess — a long passphrase does that on its own, and does it far better. They're there so that I must have forgotten one but don't know which is a believable thing for you to say.

Nobody can check your answers. There's no record anywhere of which day you feel safest, or which place, so there's nothing to catch you out against. And you don't have to invent a story and hold it under pressure: give the right place on a slightly wrong day and the app just doesn't open, exactly as it would if you'd honestly misremembered. You only have to be a little bit wrong.
What if the pattern next to Help looks different?

Then someone has set this up again using their own answers. That's the only thing that changes it.

Anything you wrote after they did that was locked to them rather than to you — they can open those, and you can't. Anything you wrote before it still belongs to the answers you were using at the time, so keep those, and don't mix them up with anything new.

To take it back, use the button in Can I forget or change my secrets later, or delete them now? above, then set it up again. That protects what you write from now on. It doesn't reach back to what's already been written.

Nothing shows afterwards. The app asks the three questions again, which is what it does by itself every so often in any case.

Whether this is a good moment to do any of that is yours to judge. The pattern changing tells you something about your situation, not just about your phone.

How far does that actually protect me?
This protects what you wrote. Someone who doesn't believe you can keep asking — but you have an endless supply of answers that sound right and aren't.

It also doesn't hide the app. What's private is what you wrote, not the fact that you wrote something.

And the person most likely to pressure you is often the person most likely to know your safe day and your safe place. If that's your situation, pick one or more answers they wouldn't expect, even if they feel slightly less true to you. The questions are prompts, not a test — nothing checks whether your answer is sincere. You might feel safe at a friend's house, but pick the location of the shop near their house that you'd sometimes go to. The possibilities are endless, and just knowing you might have picked one is part of what keeps you safe.
